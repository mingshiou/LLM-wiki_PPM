## Slide 1: Title Slide (Minimalist Cover)
* **Slide Title**: Biological Control of Bacterial Spot: Multi-Site Suppression by *Bacillus*
* **Slide Subtitle**: Direct Antibiosis, Ecological Niche Competition, and Induced Systemic Resistance in Solanaceous Crops
* **Target Audience**: Undergraduate & Graduate Plant Pathology, Applied Microbiology, and IPM Courses

### Speaker Lecture Script
> "Welcome everyone to today's lecture on the biological control of Bacterial Spot in tomato and pepper, focusing on the multi-site suppression mechanisms deployed by beneficial *Bacillus* species. 
> 
> Bacterial spot is one of the most historically damaging diseases in vegetable agriculture, driven by a complex of four distinct *Xanthomonas* species. For decades, production relied almost exclusively on preventative sprays of copper bactericides. However, widespread plasmid-borne copper resistance (*copLAB*) has rendered conventional chemical sprays increasingly ineffective, leading to devastating yield collapses under humid epidemic conditions. 
> 
> In this session, we will explore why *Bacillus*—and in particular *Bacillus velezensis*—represents the premier biological countermeasure. We will dissect its tripartite mechanism: first, direct non-ribosomal antibiotic synthesis that lyses pathogen membranes; second, competitive ecological pre-emption that starves and blocks *Xanthomonas* at stomatal entry ports; and third, root-mediated Induced Systemic Resistance (ISR) that primes whole-plant immunity across the vascular network. Let us begin."

## Slide 3: The Pathosystem & The Chemical Control Crisis
* **Layout Archetype**: `Archetype 5: .contrast-grid` (Chemical Failure vs. Biological Opportunity)
* **Citation**: (Potnis et al., 2015; Osdaghi et al., 2021)

### Speaker Lecture Script
> "Slide 3 sets the agricultural and epidemiological stage. Bacterial spot of tomato and pepper is caused by four distinct *Xanthomonas* species: *X. euvesicatoria*, *X. perforans*, *X. vesicatoria*, and *X. gardneri*. When hot, rainy seasons arrive in production epicenters like Florida, Brazil, and Southeast Asia, foliar blighting and fruit scabbing cause marketable yield losses between 23% and 44%.
> 
> For over half a century, growers responded with intensive calendar sprays of fixed copper bactericides. This relentless chemical pressure selected for the conjugative plasmid-borne *copLAB* operon. Under this system, periplasmic CopA oxidases detoxify cytotoxic Cu⁺ ions into less toxic Cu²⁺, allowing *Xanthomonas* to multiply in concentrations exceeding 200 ppm of metallic copper.
> 
> *Bacillus* provides a sustainable exit from this chemical dead-end. Unlike single-site antibiotics or heavy metals, *Bacillus* produces highly resilient endospores that survive industrial processing, UV radiation, and storage, while attacking the pathogen across multiple physiological targets simultaneously, preventing resistance selection."

## Slide 4: Phylogenomics of Biocontrol Bacilli
* **Layout Archetype**: `Archetype 1: .matrix-table` (5-Column Comparison Matrix across 4 Taxa)
* **Citation**: (Dunlap et al., 2016; Rabbee et al., 2023; Grady et al., 2019)

### Speaker Lecture Script
> "A frequent point of taxonomic confusion in the literature concerns the exact identity of biocontrol bacilli. In older papers, commercial strains such as QST713 (Serenade) or FZB42 (RhizoVital) were cataloged as *Bacillus subtilis* or *Bacillus amyloliquefaciens* subsp. *plantarum*. 
> 
> In 2016, Dunlap and colleagues conducted whole-genome Average Nucleotide Identity (ANI) and digital DNA-DNA hybridization analyses, establishing that these plant-associated strains represent a single conspecific taxon: *Bacillus velezensis*.
> 
> As you can see in the matrix table, *B. velezensis* possesses a compact circular chromosome of approximately 3.96 megabases with zero plasmids. Remarkably, between 10% and 12% of its entire genome is dedicated to 13 distinct biosynthetic gene clusters (BGCs) encoding non-ribosomal peptide synthetases (NRPS) and polyketide synthases (PKS). This biosynthetic density far exceeds that of *B. subtilis* (4%–5%), explaining why *B. velezensis* is the premier workhorse of industrial biofungicides."

## Slide 5: Direct Antibiosis: Non-Ribosomal Metabolite Arsenal
* **Layout Archetype**: `Archetype 4: .focus-mechanism` (Full-Width Vector Schematic of Xanthomonas Envelope Lysis)
* **Citation**: (Rabbee et al., 2023; Grady et al., 2019)

### Speaker Lecture Script
> "Slide 5 illustrates the multi-target molecular mechanism of direct antibiosis. When *Bacillus velezensis* encounters *Xanthomonas* in the apoplast or phyllosphere, it releases a cocktail of non-ribosomal metabolites that target the Gram-negative bacterial envelope.
> 
> First, cyclic lipopeptides (CLPs)—predominantly the Surfactin and Iturin families—act as amphiphilic detergents. Their hydrophobic lipid tails insert into the inner phospholipid bilayer, assembling into toroidal pores that cause immediate potassium efflux, loss of membrane potential (ΔΨ), and osmotic shock.
> 
> Second, antibacterial polyketides like Difficidin diffuse across the cell envelope to bind prokaryotic ribosomes, stalling translation elongation and halting cell division.
> 
> Third, the dipeptide antibiotic Bacilysin acts as a metabolic Trojan horse. Once transported across the inner membrane, host intracellular peptidases cleave Bacilysin to release anticapsin, which irreversibly inactivates glucosamine-6-phosphate (GlcN6P) synthase. This completely halts the synthesis of peptidoglycan monomers, preventing cell wall repair and resulting in catastrophic cell lysis."

## Slide 6: Rapid Bactericidal Kinetics: The GF267 Benchmark
* **Layout Archetype**: `Archetype 3: .flow-progression` (5-Hour Kill Curve SVG + Inhibition Zone Panels)
* **Citation**: (Mates et al., 2019)

### Speaker Lecture Script
> "How fast does this direct bactericidal activity occur in practice? Slide 6 showcases empirical data from Mates et al. (2019), investigating *B. velezensis* strain GF267 against *Xanthomonas perforans* isolated from tomato.
> 
> In liquid co-culture broths starting from an initial pathogen density of 1.6 × 10⁷ CFU/mL, GF267 causes an immediate, exponential collapse in viable *Xanthomonas* cells. Within two hours, populations drop by over three orders of magnitude. By hour 5, viable pathogen counts drop below the limit of detection—achieving 100% bactericidal eradication.
> 
> On solid agar, GF267 secretes diffusible metabolites producing an 8-millimeter clear radial inhibition zone. Electron micrographs confirm rapid outer membrane blebbing, cellular corrugation, and cytoplasmic leakage within 120 minutes of exposure."

## Slide 7: Ecological Niche Exclusion & Stomatal Pre-emption
* **Layout Archetype**: `Archetype 2: .diagram-split` (Stomatal Biofilm Shield SVG + NOI Analysis)
* **Citation**: (Mates et al., 2019; Kaur et al., 2026)

### Speaker Lecture Script
> "The second pillar of biocontrol is ecological niche exclusion. Microbes on the leaf surface do not exist in isolation; they compete aggressively for limited physical space and nutritional resources.
> 
> Using Biolog GN2 and GP2 microplates, researchers determined that *B. velezensis* and *X. perforans* have a reciprocal Niche Overlap Index (NOI) between 0.8 and 1.0. This means that both species utilize nearly identical carbon and nitrogen pools, including amino acids, dicarboxylic acids, and simple sugars. By colonizing the phylloplane first, *Bacillus* rapidly depletes these nutrients, essentially starving epiphytic *Xanthomonas*.
> 
> Physically, *Bacillus* secretes an extracellular biofilm matrix anchored by TasA amyloid fibers and exopolysaccharides. As seen in the cross-section diagram on the left, this biofilm forms a physical plug directly over stomatal guard cells. When invading *Xanthomonas* cells swim toward open stomata via flagellar chemotaxis, they find the entry aperture completely blocked."

## Slide 8: Decoupling Antibiosis from ISR: The Spatial Separation Assay
* **Layout Archetype**: `Archetype 3: .flow-progression` (3-Stage Experimental Pipeline: Root Inoculation → Signaling → Foliar Challenge)
* **Citation**: (Mates et al., 2019)

### Speaker Lecture Script
> "Slide 8 addresses one of the most critical experimental challenges in plant pathology: How can an investigator prove that disease reduction is caused by plant-mediated systemic resistance rather than direct microbial killing?
> 
> The answer lies in the **Spatial Separation Assay**. In this protocol, tomato seedlings at the 4-leaf stage receive 8 mL of *B. velezensis* GF267 suspension applied strictly to the potting substrate surrounding the root crown on Day 0. Physical shields ensure zero splash or aerosol contact with the foliage.
> 
> For 72 hours (Days 1–3), the plant undergoes a systemic lag phase during which root colonization triggers vascular signaling via the jasmonic acid, ethylene, and salicylic acid pathways. On Day 3, the upper 3rd and 4th leaves are challenged with a foliar spray of *X. perforans*. 
> 
> Even though the antagonist and pathogen never physically meet, foliar disease severity and the Area Under the Disease Progress Curve (AUDPC) are suppressed by 58%. This provides definitive, incontrovertible proof of Induced Systemic Resistance (ISR)."

## Slide 9: Systemic Defense Priming & Foliar Enzymatic Surges
* **Layout Archetype**: `Archetype 4: .focus-mechanism` (Full-Width Root-to-Shoot Vascular Priming & Enzyme Surge)
* **Citation**: (Mates et al., 2019; Keawmanee et al., 2025; Chang et al., 2019)

### Speaker Lecture Script
> "Now let us examine the biochemical mechanisms operating within the distal primed leaves. A common misconception is that beneficial rhizobacteria trigger massive constitutive production of defense proteins. In reality, that would impose an energetically wasteful fitness cost.
> 
> Instead, *Bacillus* establishes a state of **priming**. The plant remains phenotypically normal until pathogen attack occurs. Upon *Xanthomonas* challenge, the primed foliage mounts an accelerated defense response:
> 
> 1. **Peroxidase (PO)**: PO activity spikes by 3 days after infection, driving an intense reactive oxygen species (ROS) burst and catalyzing rapid lignin cross-linking in cell walls.
> 2. **Polyphenoloxidase (PPO)**: PPO surges simultaneously, oxidizing host phenolic compounds into antimicrobial o-quinones that cross-link and denature bacterial virulence proteins.
> 3. **Transcriptional Upregulation**: *PR-1* (pathogenesis-related protein 1) expression increases by up to 23.7-fold, while *PR-2* (beta-1,3-glucanase) increases by 4.5-fold.
> 4. **Chlorophyll Retention**: SPAD-502 measurements show that primed leaves maintain high chlorophyll levels, preventing disease-induced chlorosis and canopy defoliation."

## Slide 10: Synergistic IPM: Bioinoculants Combined with PRIs
* **Layout Archetype**: `Archetype 5: .contrast-grid` (Multi-Tiered Triad vs. Synergy & Agronomic Performance)
* **Citation**: (Keawmanee et al., 2025; Osdaghi et al., 2021)

### Speaker Lecture Script
> "In agricultural fields, relying on a single biological agent can occasionally yield variable control due to environmental fluctuations. Slide 10 demonstrates how modern integrated pest management (IPM) overcomes this through synergistic combinations with Plant Resistance Inducers (PRIs).
> 
> Keawmanee et al. (2025) evaluated bell pepper (*Capsicum annuum*) protection by pairing *B. velezensis* strain CH6 with chemical defense activators including Acibenzolar-S-methyl (ASM / BTH), beta-aminobutyric acid (BABA), and Chitosan.
> 
> Two major synergies emerged:
> First, high doses of ASM often induce a vegetative growth lag due to metabolic resource allocation. However, combining half-rate ASM (0.25 mM) with root *Bacillus* drench eliminated the growth penalty while achieving superior disease suppression.
> Second, while chemical elicitor sprays provide only a 3- to 5-day transient protection window, continuous root colonization by *Bacillus* maintains host priming throughout the growing season, suppressing bacterial leaf spot from 71.7% down to under 20%."

## Slide 11: The Tripartite Biocontrol Framework (Synthesis & Recommendations)
* **Layout Archetype**: `Archetype 2: .diagram-split` (Tripartite Biological Triangle SVG + 4 Golden Rules)
* **Citation**: (Mates et al., 2019; Rabbee et al., 2023)

### Speaker Lecture Script
> "Slide 11 brings our mechanistic journey together into a cohesive management model: the **Tripartite Biocontrol Framework**.
> 
> Resilient control of bacterial spot does not depend on a single silver bullet. It stands upon three mutually reinforcing pillars:
> - **Top Vertex**: Direct Antibiosis (surface killing via cyclic lipopeptides, difficidin, and bacilysin).
> - **Bottom Left Vertex**: Ecological Niche Exclusion (starving *Xanthomonas* via NOI 0.8–1.0 and sealing stomata with TasA biofilms).
> - **Bottom Right Vertex**: Systemic Immune Priming (vascular activation of peroxidase, polyphenoloxidase, and PR defense genes).
> 
> In commercial practice, remember the **Four Golden Rules**:
> 1. *Apply Early and Preventively*: Inoculate root crowns at seedling emergence or transplanting before pathogen immigration.
> 2. *Maintain Biofilm Viability*: Ensure soil moisture during establishment and avoid tank-mixing with harsh soil sterilants.
> 3. *Use Integrated Combinations*: Pair root-applied bacilli with foliar defense activators like BABA or Chitosan.
> 4. *Rely on High-Titer Endospores*: Use stabilized wettable powder (WP) or suspension concentrate (SC) formulations guaranteeing stability against UV and heat."
