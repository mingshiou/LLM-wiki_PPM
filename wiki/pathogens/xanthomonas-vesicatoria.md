# Xanthomonas vesicatoria

## Overview
*Xanthomonas vesicatoria* (formerly *Xanthomonas campestris* pv. *vesicatoria* group B) is a distinct species causing [[bacterial-spot-tomato-pepper]] on tomato (*[[solanum-lycopersicum]]*) and pepper (*[[capsicum-annuum]]*). It is phylogenetically separate from *[[xanthomonas-euvesicatoria]]* (group A) and *[[xanthomonas-perforans]]* (group C), displaying unique physiological characteristics such as strong amylolytic activity and distinct fatty acid profiles. *X. vesicatoria* is widespread across South America, South Africa, Eastern Europe, and Oceania.

## Taxonomic History & Reclassifications
- **Current Classification**: *Xanthomonas vesicatoria* (ex Doidge 1920) Vauterin et al. 1995 emend. Jones et al. 2004.
- **Historical Synonyms & Nomenclature Changes**:
  - Doidge (1920) initially identified *Bacterium vesicatorium* from diseased tomato in South Africa.
  - When Dye (1978) consolidated the genus under *X. campestris*, all bacterial spot xanthomonads were named *X. campestris* pv. *vesicatoria*.
  - Stall et al. (1994) partitioned strains into Group A and Group B. Group B strains were amylase-positive, serologically distinguishable, and showed lower DNA-DNA relatedness to Group A strains.
  - In 1995, Vauterin et al. revived *Xanthomonas vesicatoria* as an independent species specifically for Group B strains.
  - Jones et al. (2004) reaffirmed this species boundary using polyphasic polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis, AFLP, and DNA hybridization.

## Biology & Morphology
- **Cellular & Colony Traits**: Gram-negative, motile rod, single polar flagellum. Forms yellow-pigmented (xanthomonadin), glistening colonies on standard nutrient media.
- **Physiology**: Strongly amylolytic (hydrolyzes starch agar, contrasting with *X. euvesicatoria* which is amylase-negative).
- **Environmental Adaptation & Geography**: Prevalent in cooler and temperate solanaceous crop regions globally, but also identified in subtropical zones in South America and Africa.

## Molecular Pathogenicity & Virulence Factors
- **Type III Secretion System (T3SS)**: Possesses the canonical *hrp* cluster delivering effector proteins to plant cells.
- **Effector Repertoire**:
  - Features conserved non-TALE effectors including **[[avrbs2]]**, crucial for establishing full apoplastic bacterial population levels.
  - TALE repertoires differ from *X. euvesicatoria*: *X. vesicatoria* typically lacks canonical *avrBs3*, meaning it does not trigger the *Bs3*-mediated hypersensitive response in pepper lines that carry the *Bs3* resistance gene.
  - Encodes distinct combinations of Xop family effectors (e.g., XopN, XopQ, XopX) that attenuate basal immune signaling.

## Associated Diseases
- **[[bacterial-spot-tomato-pepper]]**: Causes angular to circular water-soaked lesions on leaves and stems, turning brown to black and necrotic, with premature leaf shedding and blister-like scab formation on fruit.

## Key Research Protocols & Model Strains
- **Model Strain ATCC 35937 / LMG 911**: Standard reference strain used in taxonomic typing, comparative genomics, and diagnostic marker development.
