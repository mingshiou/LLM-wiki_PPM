# Acidovorax citrulli

## Overview
*Acidovorax citrulli* (formerly *Acidovorax avenae* subsp. *citrulli*) is a Gram-negative, rod-shaped, seedborne phytopathogenic bacterium belonging to the Betaproteobacteria. It is the causal agent of **[[bacterial-fruit-blotch]] (BFB)**, one of the most economically devastating diseases affecting cucurbit crops worldwide, particularly watermelon (*[[citrullus-lanatus]]*) and melon (*Cucumis melo*). The bacterium invades seeds, seedlings, foliage, and fruit rinds, producing water-soaked lesions that progress to extensive fruit decay and complete field crop loss.

## Taxonomic History & Reclassifications
- **Current Classification**: *Acidovorax citrulli* (Schaad et al. 1978) Schaad et al. 2008.
- **Historical Synonyms & Sequence of Nomenclature Changes**:
  - **1978**: Originally isolated from plant introductions of watermelon in Florida and described by Schaad et al. as ***Pseudomonas pseudoalcaligenes* subsp. *citrulli*** based on physiological and nutritional characteristics.
  - **1992**: Willems et al. carried out extensive rRNA-DNA hybridization and phenotypic restructuring of the genus *Pseudomonas*, transferring this organism along with related phytopathogenic and environmental pseudomonads to the newly erected genus *Acidovorax* within the Comamonadaceae family, designating it ***Acidovorax avenae* subsp. *citrulli***.
  - **2008**: Following polyphasic taxonomic investigations—including DNA-DNA hybridization, amplified fragment length polymorphism (AFLP) DNA fingerprinting, and host range specialization—Schaad et al. formally elevated the three subspecies of *A. avenae* to standalone species rank:
    - *Acidovorax avenae* (restricted to monocots/grasses: oats, corn, rice, sugarcane)
    - *Acidovorax cattleyae* (restricted to orchids)
    - ***Acidovorax citrulli*** comb. nov. (specialized on Cucurbitaceae).
- **Population Genetic Subgroups**:
  - Global strains of *A. citrulli* are partitioned into two distinct phylogenetic lineages (Group I and Group II):
    - **Group I**: Characterized by moderate genetic diversity; primarily isolated from melon (*Cucumis melo*) and non-watermelon cucurbits.
    - **Group II**: Highly clonal and genetically homogeneous; predominantly isolated from watermelon (*Citrullus lanatus*) and responsible for the major destructive pandemics across the Americas, Asia, and Europe.

## Biology & Morphology
- **Cellular Morphology**: Gram-negative, strictly aerobic, non-spore-forming straight rod (0.5 × 1.0–2.0 $\mu\text{m}$) propelled by a single polar flagellum.
- **Colony Appearance**: Smooth, circular, white to cream-colored, non-pigmented (or pale yellowish-buff on nutrient-rich media), non-fluorescent on King's B agar (distinguishing it from fluorescent *Pseudomonas* species).
- **Physiology**: Oxidase-positive, catalase-positive, accumulates poly-$\beta$-hydroxybutyrate (PHB) intracellularly, does not hydrolyze starch.
- **Epidemiology & Seedborne Cycle**:
  - Seeds are the primary vehicle for survival, long-distance dissemination, and seasonal epidemics. Even trace seed contamination levels (<1 in 10,000 seeds) can trigger 100% field incidence under overhead irrigation and temperatures above 28°C–30°C.
  - Bacteria enter through stomata, hydathodes, or micro-wounds on cotyledons during germination, colonizing the spongy mesophyll apoplast.

## Molecular Pathogenicity & Virulence Factors
- **Type III Secretion System (T3SS)**:
  - Encoded by a conserved *hrp* / *hrc* pathogenicity island essential for pathogenicity on cucurbits and elicitation of the hypersensitive response (HR) on non-hosts (e.g., *Nicotiana benthamiana*).
  - T3SS mutants fail to colonize watermelon mesophyll and cannot establish fruit blotch symptoms.
- **Type IV Pili (T4P)**:
  - Required for twitching motility, surface adhesion, and biofilm development on seed coats and fruit cuticle. Mutants lacking functional *pil* genes exhibit severely reduced seed transmission and host tissue ingress.
- **Type II Secretion System (T2SS) & Cell Wall Degrading Enzymes**:
  - Secretes polygalacturonases, pectate lyases, and endoglucanases that macerate cucurbit fruit rinds, leading to the collapse and crater-like rotting of fruit tissues.
- **EPS & Biofilm Matrix**:
  - Abundant extracellular polysaccharide production mediates survival against desiccation during seed storage.

## Associated Diseases
- **[[bacterial-fruit-blotch]]**: Destructive water-soaked lesions on watermelon and melon leaves; greasy, dark olive-green blotches on mature fruit rinds that crack and collapse with internal fermentation.

## Key Research Strains
- **Aac153 / Aac153r**: Virulent Group II model strain isolated in Taiwan/Southeast Asia, frequently utilized in seed treatment and host immunity research.
- **Aac00-1**: Fully sequenced Group II reference strain (Georgia, USA) used globally in comparative genomics.
