# Xanthomonas euvesicatoria

## Overview
*Xanthomonas euvesicatoria* (formerly *Xanthomonas campestris* pv. *vesicatoria* group A; also designated ***Xanthomonas euvesicatoria* pv. *euvesicatoria***) is a major Gram-negative, rod-shaped phytopathogenic bacterium causing [[bacterial-spot-tomato-pepper]] on solanaceous crops, primarily tomato (*[[solanum-lycopersicum]]*) and pepper (*[[capsicum-annuum]]*). It is characterized by bright yellow, mucoid colonies produced by brominated aryl-polyene pigments (**xanthomonadins**) and xanthan exopolysaccharide. *X. euvesicatoria* utilizes a conserved hrp-encoded Type III Secretion System (T3SS) to translocate over 30 effector proteins directly into host plant cells, subverting host immunity and driving apoplastic colonization.

## Taxonomic History & Reclassifications
- **Current Nomenclature Status**: *Xanthomonas euvesicatoria* Jones et al. 2004 / 2006. In revised phylogenomic classifications, it is designated as ***Xanthomonas euvesicatoria* pv. *euvesicatoria*** (Constantin et al., 2016).
- **Historical Synonyms & Sequence of Nomenclature Changes**:
  - **1920**: First described by Ethel Doidge in South Africa as *Bacterium vesicatorium* from diseased tomato seedlings, and simultaneously documented in Indiana, USA by Gardner and Kendrick.
  - **1939**: Transferred to *Xanthomonas vesicatoria* by Dowson.
  - **1978**: Dye consolidated xanthomonads into pathovars under *Xanthomonas campestris*, designating this organism *Xanthomonas campestris* pv. *vesicatoria* (*Xcv*).
  - **1994**: Stall et al. demonstrated that *Xcv* comprised two distinct entities: Group A (non-amylolytic, sensitive to specific phages) and Group B (amylolytic).
  - **1995**: Vauterin et al. designated Group A as *Xanthomonas axonopodis* pv. *vesicatoria*.
  - **2004 / 2006**: Jones et al. applied polyphasic DNA-DNA hybridization, AFLP, and FAME analyses, elevating Group A to species rank: ***Xanthomonas euvesicatoria*** sp. nov.
  - **2016**: Phylogenomic average nucleotide identity (ANI) studies by Constantin et al. united *X. euvesicatoria* and *X. perforans* under the single species *X. euvesicatoria*, establishing the autonym pathovar ***Xanthomonas euvesicatoria* pv. *euvesicatoria***.
- **Model Strain Benchmark**: Literature spanning 1980–2020 prominently features reference strain **85-10** (Thieme et al., 2005), which serves as the global gold standard for T3SS and TALE characterization.

## Biology & Morphology
- **Cellular Morphology**: Gram-negative, motile rod (0.6–0.7 × 1.0–2.4 μm) with a single polar flagellum.
- **Colony Appearance**: Domed, pale yellow colonies on nutrient agar due to xanthomonadin, with copious xanthan slime.
- **Physiology**: Obligate aerobe, catalase-positive, oxidase-negative. Unlike *[[xanthomonas-vesicatoria]]* and *[[xanthomonas-perforans]]*, *X. euvesicatoria* is typically non-amylolytic (fails to hydrolyze starch), although rare atypical amylolytic field strains have been identified in Nigeria (Jibrin et al., 2015).
- **Epidemiology & Seed Transmission**: Survives in infested crop debris, volunteer solanaceous weeds (*Solanum americanum*, *Physalis pubescens*), and commercial seed lots. Infection is initiated by splashing water or contaminated transplant handling; bacteria penetrate stomata, hydathodes, or micro-wounds and colonize the apoplast.

## Molecular Pathogenicity & Virulence Factors
- **Type III Secretion System (T3SS)**: Encoded by the 23-kb *hrp* / *hrc* gene cluster organized into six transcriptional units (*hrpA* to *hrpF*), master-regulated by HrpG (response regulator) and HrpX (AraC-type activator).
  - **Secretion Hierarchy**: T3S chaperone HpaB acts as an escort; inner membrane protein HrcU acts as a switch coordinating early substrate translocation (HrpB2, HpaC) and late effector injection (Hausner & Büttner, 2014; Scheibner et al., 2017).
- **Major Effectors (T3SEs)**:
  - **[[avrbs2]]**: Essential core virulence factor possessing a glycerophosphoryl diester phosphodiesterase (GPD) domain; targeted by pepper CC-NB-LRR Bs2. In field populations under *Bs2* pressure, *X. euvesicatoria* evolved point mutations resulting in single amino acid substitutions that evade Bs2 recognition without suffering the catastrophic fitness cost observed in laboratory deletion mutants (Gassmann et al., 2000).
  - **[[avrbs3]]**: Prototypical TALE binding host promoter elements (e.g., *upa20*) to induce cell hypertrophy; recognized by pepper executor *Bs3*.
  - **XopAU**: Recently discovered via machine learning; a catalytically active serine/threonine protein kinase that manipulates host immune signaling by directly phosphorylating plant MAPK receptor kinase MKK2 (Teper et al., 2016).
  - **XopL**: Novel E3 ubiquitin ligase that colocalizes with host plant microtubules independent of its enzymatic activity, suppressing stromule formation and nuclear trafficking of plastids (Singer et al., 2013; Erickson et al., 2018).
  - **AvrBsT (XopJ2)**: Acetylates plant microtubule-associated protein ACIP1, disrupting host cytoskeletal dynamics and immune signaling.
- **Antimicrobial Resistance Discovery**:
  - The first discovery of plasmid-mediated copper resistance in phytopathogenic bacteria was made in *X. euvesicatoria* in Florida (Marco & Stall, 1983). The resistance is encoded by conjugative *copLAB* plasmids (see [[copper-bactericides-and-resistance]]).

## Associated Diseases
- **[[bacterial-spot-tomato-pepper]]**: Causes water-soaked to dark brown greasy necrotic spots on foliage, defoliation, stem cankers, and wart-like scabs on fruit.

## Key Research Protocols & Model Strains
- **Model Strain 85-10**: Sequenced reference strain (Thieme et al., 2005) used to dissect T3SS structure, effector translocation kinetics, and host immune perception.
