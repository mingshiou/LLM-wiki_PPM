# Xanthomonas gardneri

## Overview
*Xanthomonas gardneri* (formerly *Xanthomonas campestris* pv. *vesicatoria* group D; also classified as *Xanthomonas hortorum* pv. *gardneri*) is one of the four causative agents of [[bacterial-spot-tomato-pepper]]. It infects both tomato (*[[solanum-lycopersicum]]*) and pepper (*[[capsicum-annuum]]*). Notably, *X. gardneri* thrives in cooler, temperate, or highland climates where other bacterial spot xanthomonads are often constrained, making it a prominent threat in the Midwestern United States, Canada, Central Europe, Brazil, and mountainous regions of Central America.

## Taxonomic History & Reclassifications
- **Current Nomenclature Status**: *Xanthomonas gardneri* (ex Šutić 1957) Jones et al. 2004 / 2006. In contemporary phylogenomic classifications, it is designated as ***Xanthomonas hortorum* pv. *gardneri*** (Morinière et al., 2020).
- **Historical Synonyms & Sequence of Nomenclature Changes**:
  - **1957**: Originally described by Šutić in Yugoslavia as *Pseudomonas gardneri* based on a bacterial spot outbreak on tomato.
  - **1978**: Reduced to a synonym under *Xanthomonas campestris* pv. *vesicatoria* during the pathovar consolidation.
  - **1990s**: Re-recognized by Stall and colleagues as **Group D** among bacterial spot strains, distinguishable by amylolytic activity and distinct fatty acid profiles.
  - **2004 / 2006**: Jones et al. formally elevated Group D to species status as ***Xanthomonas gardneri*** sp. nov.
  - **2019**: Timilsina, Kara, et al. demonstrated that the average nucleotide identity (ANI) between representative genomes of *X. gardneri* and the artichoke pathogen *Xanthomonas cynarae* was well above the 95%–96% species threshold, reclassifying it as a junior heterotypic synonym: ***Xanthomonas cynarae* pv. *gardneri***.
  - **2020**: Morinière et al. established through comprehensive phylogenomics that *Xanthomonas cynarae* itself is a junior heterotypic synonym of the garden crop pathogen *Xanthomonas hortorum* Vauterin et al. 1995. Consequently, the pathogen was transferred to ***Xanthomonas hortorum* pv. *gardneri***.
- **Current Usage**: While European phytosanitary authorities and phylogenomic literature adopt *X. hortorum* pv. *gardneri*, the binominal *Xanthomonas gardneri* remains widely employed across American phytopathology and seed certification databases.

## Biology & Morphology
- **Cellular & Colony Traits**: Gram-negative rod, polar flagellum, pale yellow xanthomonadin pigmentation on nutrient agar.
- **Physiological Traits**: Strongly amylolytic (starch-hydrolyzing); nonpectolytic in standard assays.
- **Thermal Adaptation & Epidemiology**:
  - Exhibits lower optimum growth temperatures compared to *[[xanthomonas-perforans]]* and *[[xanthomonas-euvesicatoria]]*, causing severe epidemics during cool, wet spring seasons.
  - **Remarkable Clonal Monomorphism**: Multilocus sequence analysis (MLSA) revealed that a single multilocus haplotype is distributed worldwide with zero nucleotide sequence divergence between the original 1953 Yugoslavian type strain and 2010s isolates from Canada, the USA, Costa Rica, Brazil, Ethiopia, and Reunion Island. This absolute conservation indicates a very recent, rapid global dissemination via international hybrid seed trade.

## Molecular Pathogenicity & Virulence Factors
- **Type III Secretion System (T3SS)**: Conserved *hrp* pathogenicity island regulating effector injection into the host apoplast.
- **Key Effector Determinants**:
  - **[[avrbs2]]**: Highly conserved core effector necessary for full apoplastic colonization.
  - **AvrHah1**: A major Transcription Activator-Like Effector (TALE) originally discovered in *X. gardneri*. AvrHah1 upregulates two host basic helix-loop-helix (bHLH) transcription factors in tomato and pepper, which in turn drive massive transcriptional activation of host pectate lyase genes. This induction causes severe water-soaking in foliar tissues, facilitating rapid bacterial proliferation and apoplastic dispersal.
  - **Antimicrobial Resistance**: Strains from Illinois and South America frequently harbor plasmid-mediated copper resistance (*cop* operons) and streptomycin resistance, posing major management challenges (see [[copper-bactericides-and-resistance]]).

## Associated Diseases
- **[[bacterial-spot-tomato-pepper]]**: Causes circular to irregular brown-black lesions on foliage and rough, scabby fruit craters.

## Key Research Protocols & Model Strains
- **Model Strain ICMP 7383 / ATCC 19865 / LMG 8004**: Yugoslavian type strain characterized in phylogenomic comparisons and host differential assays.
