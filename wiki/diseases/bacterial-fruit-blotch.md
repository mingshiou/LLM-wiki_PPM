# Bacterial Fruit Blotch (BFB)

## Overview
**Bacterial Fruit Blotch (BFB)**, incited by the seedborne Betaproteobacterium *[[acidovorax-citrulli]]*, is the most destructive and feared bacterial disease affecting cucurbit crops, particularly watermelon (*[[citrullus-lanatus]]*) and cantaloupe / melon (*Cucumis melo*). First detected in commercial fields in the late 1980s in the United States, BFB rapidly spread via international seed commerce, causing catastrophic losses approaching 100% of marketable yields in major cucurbit-growing regions of the Americas, Asia, Europe, and Australia. The disease damages seedlings and foliage, but derives its notoriety from late-stage symptoms on mature fruit, where large, greasy, oil-soaked blotches ruin the rind and liquefy the internal flesh.

## Symptomology & Progression
- **Seedlings and Cotyledons**:
  - Initial symptoms emerge on newly germinated seedlings as water-soaked lesions on the lower (abaxial) surfaces of cotyledons, often following the vascular veins.
  - Lesions rapidly become dark, necrotic, and coalesce into blights; seedlings collapse and damp off under high relative humidity (>80%) and temperatures exceeding 28°C.
- **True Leaves & Stems**:
  - Lesions on true foliage are small (1–5 mm), angular to irregular, and reddish-brown to dark brown, frequently bordered by a faint chlorotic halo.
  - Although foliar symptoms can appear deceptively mild, infected leaves serve as a continuous reservoir producing millions of bacterial cells that splash onto flowers and developing fruit.
- **Fruit Symptoms (The Hallmark Blotch)**:
  - Symptoms on fruit typically appear shortly before harvest, beginning as small (few millimeters), dark green, greasy, water-soaked spots on the upper exposed sunlit surface of the rind.
  - Within days, these spots expand rapidly into irregular, oil-soaked, dark olive-green **"blotches"** that can cover the entire upper half of the melon.
  - The cuticle over the blotch ruptures, releasing amber-colored bacterial ooze. Secondary opportunistic soft-rot bacteria invade the cracks, rapidly decomposing internal melon pulp into a foul-smelling liquid mass.

## Disease Cycle & Epidemiology
1. **Primary Inoculum via Infested Seeds**:
   - *Acidovorax citrulli* survives externally on the seed coat and internally beneath the seed coat and within the embryo. Seed transmission is extraordinarily efficient: a single contaminated seed per 10,000 can initiate a devastating field epidemic.
2. **Greenhouse Transplant Amplification**:
   - Commercial seedling production trays create an optimal incubator: dense canopies, warm temperatures (28°C–32°C), and daily overhead misting facilitate explosive mechanical and aerosol transmission from a single latent seedling to thousands of surrounding transplants.
3. **Field Dispersal**:
   - Secondary spread occurs via wind-driven rain droplets, irrigation splash, and agricultural machinery.
   - Blossom infection during pollination provides the primary route for bacteria to invade developing seed cavities in fruit.

## Management & Control Strategies

### 1. Seed Health & Sanitation
- **Seed Health Testing**: Mandatory testing of commercial seed lots using seed wash PCR, immunodiagnostics, or grow-out seedling assays (with zero-tolerance thresholds).
- **Physical & Chemical Disinfestation**: Dry heat treatment (85°C for 3–5 days) or hot water soaks (52°C for 15–20 min), as well as peroxyacetic acid or hydrochloric acid treatments to eradicate seed coat bacterial loads.

### 2. Biological Control via Immunity Priming (PTI Intensification)
Recent breakthroughs show that biological seed treatments with plant-beneficial *Bacillus* strains effectively eradicate seedborne transmission by intensifying host **[[pamp-triggered-immunity]] (PTI)** (Chang et al., 2019):
- **Seed Soaking with *Bacillus* (PMB04 & PMB05)**:
  - Soaking *A. citrulli*-contaminated watermelon seeds in cell suspensions ($10^7\text{ CFU/mL}$) of *[[bacillus-velezensis]]* / *B. amyloliquefaciens* strains **PMB04** or **PMB05** for 24 h suppressed seedling disease incidence from **84.6% to 2.9%** and reduced disease severity index from **50.9% to 1.4%**.
  - In contrast to strains with strong in vitro antibiosis alone (such as *B. thuringiensis* DR81), PMB04 and PMB05 prime the watermelon host to execute an explosive, accelerated **Reactive Oxygen Species (ROS) oxidative burst** via plant NADPH oxidase and dense **callose deposition** around stomatal invasion courts within hours of pathogen contact, completely blocking apoplastic colonization.

### 3. Chemical Treatments
- Foliar sprays of copper bactericides (copper hydroxide, copper oxychloride) applied preventively from early flowering through fruit set can suppress bacterial spread, though efficacy drops once fruit blotches are established and copper resistance risks exist (see [[copper-bactericides-and-resistance]]).
