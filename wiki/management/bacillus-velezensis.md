# Bacillus velezensis

## Overview
*Bacillus velezensis* is an aerobic, Gram-positive, endospore-forming soil and endophytic bacterium widely deployed as a premier biological control agent (BCA) and plant growth-promoting rhizobacterium (PGPR) in sustainable agriculture. First described in 2005 from the Vélez River in Málaga, Spain, *B. velezensis* has emerged as an industrial workhorse capable of suppressing a wide spectrum of phytopathogenic fungi, oomycetes, and bacteria—including the [[bacterial-spot-tomato-pepper]] complex (*[[xanthomonas-euvesicatoria]]*, *[[xanthomonas-perforans]]*, *[[xanthomonas-vesicatoria]]*, *[[xanthomonas-gardneri]]*). Its disease suppression is mediated by multi-site antagonism via non-ribosomal lipopeptides and polyketides, competitive rhizosphere colonization, and the elicitation of host [[induced-systemic-resistance]] (ISR).

## Taxonomic History & Reclassifications
- **Species Description**: Originally circumscribed by Ruiz-García et al. (2005).
- **The BASC Complex & Synonyms**:
  - For over a decade, strains of *B. velezensis* were widely misidentified or cataloged under related species within the *Bacillus amyloliquefaciens* species complex (BASC), notably as ***Bacillus amyloliquefaciens* subsp. *plantarum*** and ***Bacillus methylotrophicus***.
  - Comprehensive phylogenomic revisions based on core-genome Average Nucleotide Identity (ANI) and digital DNA-DNA hybridization (dDDH) demonstrated that *B. amyloliquefaciens* subsp. *plantarum*, *B. methylotrophicus*, and *B. velezensis* form a single conspecific taxon, with ***Bacillus velezensis*** holding nomenclatural priority (Dunlap et al., 2016).
  - Renowned model strains and commercial biofungicide strains historically cited as *B. subtilis* or *B. amyloliquefaciens*—such as **FZB42** (RhizoVital), **QST713** (Serenade), **SQR9**, **GB03** (Kodiak), and **83** (Fungifree AB)—are now taxonomically reclassified as *Bacillus velezensis*.

## Secondary Metabolite Arsenal
Over 10% to 12% of the *B. velezensis* genome is dedicated to non-ribosomal peptide synthetases (NRPS) and polyketide synthases (PKS) governing antimicrobial biosynthesis:

### 1. Cyclic Lipopeptides (CLPs)
Synthesized by modular NRPS mega-enzymes (see [[cyclic-lipopeptides]]):
- **Surfactins** (*srfA*): Cyclic heptapeptides linked to $\beta$-hydroxy fatty acids. Potent biosurfactants facilitating biofilm spreading, root surface wetting, and membrane perturbation; primary bacterial trigger for plant [[induced-systemic-resistance]].
- **Iturins / Bacillomycin D** (*bmy* / *itu*): Cyclic heptapeptides with $\beta$-amino fatty acids. Exert potent antifungal activity against phytopathogenic fungi (*Fusarium oxysporum*, *Botrytis cinerea*) by binding sterols in fungal plasma membranes, creating leakage pores. Bacillomycin D also functions as an internal iron-scavenging signaling molecule regulating bacterial biofilm formation via the KinB-Spo0A-SinI-SinR cascade.
- **Fengycins** (*fen*): Cyclic decapeptides with $\beta$-hydroxy fatty acids. Target filamentous fungal membranes, altering lipid monolayer architecture and inducing cell death. Active against *Fusarium solani* (IC50 5.58 $\mu$g/mL, outperforming chemical fungicides thiram and hymexazol).

### 2. Antibacterial Polyketides (PKs)
Synthesized via modular PKS assembly lines:
- **Difficidin**: Highly active against Gram-negative bacterial pathogens, including *Xanthomonas* spp. and *Erwinia amylovora*. Down-regulates pathogen genes essential for cell division, protein translation, and cell wall assembly.
- **Macrolactin**: Macrocyclic lactones inhibiting bacterial protein synthesis; synergistic with lipopeptides.
- **Bacillaene**: Linear polyketide/non-ribosomal hybrid inhibiting prokaryotic protein synthesis.

### 3. Bacteriocins & Small Antimicrobial Molecules
- **Bacilysin**: Dipeptide non-ribosomal antibiotic consisting of L-alanine and anticapsin. Irreversibly inhibits **glucosamine-6-phosphate synthase**, blocking peptidoglycan biosynthesis in target bacterial cell walls. Strongly suppresses *Xanthomonas oryzae* and lyses *Phytophthora sojae* hyphae.
- **Plantazolicin & Amylocyclicin**: Ribosomally synthesized and post-translationally modified peptides (RiPPs) displaying targeted bactericidal activity.
- **Chitinases (*chiA*)**: Hydrolyze $\beta$-1,4-glycosidic bonds in fungal cell walls and insect peritrophic membranes.

### 4. Volatile Organic Compounds (VOCs)
- Emits low-molecular-weight volatiles including **2,3-butanediol**, **acetoin (3-hydroxy-2-butanone)**, dimethyl pyrazine, and benzothiazoles.
- VOCs diffuse rapidly through soil pores and plant canopies, inhibiting mycelial expansion of *Sclerotinia sclerotiorum* and triggering stomatal closure and defense priming in host leaves.

## Mechanisms of Rhizosphere Competence & Plant Protection
1. **Chemotaxis & Root Colonization**:
   - Responds to plant root exudates; host-secreted D-galactose acts as a chemoattractant sensed via methyl-accepting chemotaxis protein McpA.
   - DegS/DegU two-component regulatory phosphorylation drives matrix synthesis, while AI-2 quorum sensing (*luxS*) coordinates collective cellular density.
2. **Biofilm Architecture**:
   - Produces robust, extracellular polysaccharide (EPS) and TasA amyloid-fiber-rich biofilms that envelop roots and seed coats, forming a physical and chemical shield against soil-borne invaders.
3. **Induced Systemic Resistance (ISR)**:
   - Secretion of surfactins, acetoin, and 2,3-butanediol perceived by plant receptors triggers systemic priming without fitness penalties. Upregulates antioxidant enzymes (superoxide dismutase [SOD], catalase [CAT], peroxidase [POD]) and pathogenesis-related proteins (*PR-1*, *PR-2*, *PR-4*).
4. **Plant Growth Promotion (PGPR)**:
   - Synthesizes phytohormones: Indole-3-acetic acid (auxin, IAA) and gibberellic acid (GA3).
   - Solubilizes bound soil phosphates, fixes nitrogen (in strains like XT1), and produces iron-chelating catecholate siderophore **bacillibactin**.

## Model Strain GF267: Multi-Site Suppression of Tomato Bacterial Spot
An illustrative benchmark of multi-site biocontrol is provided by *Bacillus velezensis* strain **GF267**, isolated from agricultural rhizosphere in Brazil and evaluated against *[[xanthomonas-perforans]]* on tomato (*[[solanum-lycopersicum]]*) (Mates et al., 2019):
1. **Direct Antibiosis & In Vitro Lethality**:
   - Secretes diffusible antimicrobial metabolites producing an 8-mm inhibition halo against *X. perforans* in dual-culture agar.
   - In liquid co-culture broths, GF267 eliminates viable *X. perforans* cells within **5 hours** of inoculation (dropping bacterial counts from $1.6 \times 10^7\text{ CFU/mL}$ to 0), demonstrating rapid bactericidal activity.
2. **Preemptive Exclusion & Nutritional Niche Overlap (NOI)**:
   - Evaluated via Biolog GN2/GP2 microplates. GF267 and *X. perforans* display substantial reciprocal niche overlap indices ($\text{NOI} = 0.8\text{--}1.0$) across carbon sources (carbohydrates, amino acids, and organic acids).
   - Establishing GF267 on the phylloplane prior to pathogen challenge prevents *Xanthomonas* colonization via nutritional resource monopolization and physical occupation of stomatal entry points.
3. **Induced Systemic Resistance via Root Colonization**:
   - Demonstrated using the [[spatial-separation-isr-assay]]: application of GF267 cell suspensions ($5 \times 10^7\text{ CFU/mL}$) strictly to the root crown significantly reduced foliar bacterial spot severity and AUDPC on distant leaves by ~58%.
   - Mechanistically, GF267 anticipates plant immune activation, triggering an earlier surge in foliar **peroxidase (PO)** (associated with the initial reactive oxygen species oxidative burst) and **polyphenoloxidase (PPO)** (oxidizing phenolic compounds into antimicrobial quinones) at 3 days post-infection.
4. **Photosynthetic Maintenance**:
   - Enhances and preserves chlorophyll levels (measured via SPAD-502), mitigating disease-induced chlorosis.

## Strain CH6: Pepper Defense Activation and Indole Metabolite Enrichment
In addition to tomato trials, *B. velezensis* strain **CH6** was demonstrated to control bacterial leaf spot in bell pepper (*[[capsicum-annuum]]*) caused by *[[xanthomonas-euvesicatoria]]* (Keawmanee et al., 2025):
- **Disease Suppression**: Soil drenching with CH6 ($10^7\text{ CFU/mL}$) prior to pathogen inoculation suppressed leaf spot disease severity index from 71.7% in control plants down to 25.1% at 21 DAI.
- **Transcriptional & Metabolic Profiling**:
  - Triggers significant systemic upregulation of ***PR-1*** (23.7-fold) and ***PR-2*** (4.5-fold) in foliage, confirming that rhizospheric *B. velezensis* can stimulate the salicylic acid-dependent defense pathway.
  - GC-MS profiling revealed that CH6 root colonization uniquely enriches antimicrobial **indole derivatives** (specifically indol-2(3H)-one compounds), demonstrating that *B. velezensis* primes host secondary metabolism in addition to pathogenesis-related proteins (see [[chemical-resistance-inducers]]).

## Strains PMB04 & PMB05: Watermelon Seed Treatment and PTI Intensification
In cucurbit production, *B. velezensis* / *B. amyloliquefaciens* strains **PMB04** and **PMB05**, isolated from agricultural rhizosphere soils in Taiwan, serve as model biocontrol strains against **[[bacterial-fruit-blotch]] (BFB)** caused by *[[acidovorax-citrulli]]* on watermelon (*[[citrullus-lanatus]]*) (Chang et al., 2019):
- **Seed Treatment Efficacy**: Soaking contaminated watermelon seeds in bacterial suspensions ($10^7\text{ CFU/mL}$) of PMB04 or PMB05 for 24 h reduced seedling BFB incidence from 84.6% to 2.9%–7.5%, and reduced disease severity index from 50.9% to 1.4%–2.1%.
- **Intensification of PAMP-Triggered Immunity (PTI)**:
  - When co-inoculated with *A. citrulli* or the bacterial flagellin peptide **flg22**, PMB04 and PMB05 hyper-activate the plant immune system, amplifying the early apoplastic **Reactive Oxygen Species (ROS) oxidative burst** by up to **2,733-fold within 1 hour** (staining with $\text{H}_2\text{DCFDA}$) and boosting **callose deposition** by up to 154-fold (aniline blue fluorometry).
  - Pharmacological inhibition using diphenyleneiodonium (DPI) confirmed that plant NADPH oxidase-mediated ROS generation is indispensable for PMB05-mediated BFB disease suppression (see [[pamp-triggered-immunity]]).

## Model Strain 9D-6: Genome Architecture and Secondary Metabolome Mining
The complete genome sequencing and functional characterization of *Bacillus velezensis* strain **9D-6** (isolated from potato rhizosphere in Ontario, Canada; GenBank CP020805) provides an archetype for the genomic organization of plant-protecting bacilli (Grady et al., 2019):
- **Genomic Metric**: Single circular chromosome of **3.96 Mb** (3,959,428 bp) with 46.4% GC content, encoding 3,849 protein-coding genes and 93 RNA genes, with zero plasmids (characteristic of ~80% of *B. velezensis* strains). In silico DNA-DNA hybridization (isDDH) ranges from 79% to 92% against other *B. velezensis* genomes, confirming clear species separation from *B. amyloliquefaciens* (55%–56% isDDH against type strain DSM 7).
- **antiSMASH Secondary Metabolite Clusters (13 BGCs)**:
  - **4 NRPS Clusters**: Surfactin (820–886 kb; sequence `ELLVDLL`), Fengycin (2,364–2,490 kb), Bacillomycin D, and Bacillibactin (3,562–3,629 kb, catecholate siderophore).
  - **3 Trans-AT PKS Clusters**: Macrolactin (1,893–1,979 kb), Bacillaene (2,209–2,312 kb), and Difficidin (2,801–2,901 kb).
  - **1 Hybrid PKS/NRPS Cluster**: Bacilysin (174–216 kb).
  - **Special / Ancillary Clusters**: Butirosin (Type II PKS), Terpenes, Class II Lantipeptide (containing LanM), and a rare **ladderane / aryl-polyene cluster** (1,177–1,219 kb) providing oxidative stress protection.
- **Mass Spectrometric Identification of Active Surfactins**:
  - High-resolution LC-MS/MS and de novo sequencing identified the principal antibacterial metabolites active against phytopathogenic corynebacteria (*Clavibacter michiganensis*) as **$[Leu^7]\text{ surfactin }\text{C}_{14}$** ($\text{C}_{52}\text{H}_{91}\text{N}_7\text{O}_{13}$, surfactin B) and **$[Leu^7]\text{ surfactin }\text{C}_{15}$** ($\text{C}_{53}\text{H}_{93}\text{N}_7\text{O}_{13}$, surfactin C).
- **Non-Antagonistic Rhizosphere Exclusion & In Vivo Biocontrol**:
  - Although live 9D-6 and its purified surfactins exhibited zero in vitro growth inhibition against *Pseudomonas syringae* pv. *tomato* DC3000 on agar plates, co-inoculation on *Arabidopsis* roots resulted in an almost complete suppression of RFP-labeled DC3000 root attachment and severe disease abatement.
  - This demonstrates that *B. velezensis* protects host plants through physical root niche competition, sub-inhibitory signaling disruption, and **[[induced-systemic-resistance]] (ISR)** driven by 2,3-butanediol dehydrogenase (*bdh*) and biosurfactant spreading.

## Critical Evaluation: The "Beneficial vs. Facultative Pathogen" Debate
While *B. velezensis* has an exemplary safety record as a commercial biofertilizer and EPA/EU-approved biocontrol agent, recent academic investigations highlight isolated instances of opportunistic crop damage by specific wild isolates:
- **Onion Bulb Rot**: In South Korea, bacterial rot of stored onion bulbs was linked to an isolate initially designated *B. amyloliquefaciens* and later re-identified as *B. velezensis* (Hwang et al., 2012).
- **Potato Tuber Soft Rot**: In Guangdong, China, isolate F10-1 was recovered from decaying potato tubers exhibiting pectinolytic soft rot (Wang et al., 2017).
- **Peach Fruit Rot**: Isolate zk1 was identified as the causative agent of fruit rot in peaches (*Prunus persica*), where it disrupted host reactive oxygen scavenging enzymes (SOD, CAT, POD) and compromised mitochondrial integrity (Zeng et al., 2022).
- **Agricultural Perspective**: None of these phytopathogenic occurrences involve commercial BCA formulations. Instead, they reflect extreme laboratory inoculation pressures or natural strain divergence where specific environmental isolates carry elevated pectin lyase or protease loads. Regulators and agronomists emphasize rigorous whole-genome screening of prospective wild strains prior to agricultural field deployment.
