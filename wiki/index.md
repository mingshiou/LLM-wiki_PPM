## 1. Pathogens (`wiki/pathogens/`)
*Catalog of pathogenic fungi, oomycetes, bacteria, viruses, viroids, phytoplasmas, and nematodes.*

- [[acidovorax-citrulli]]: Gram-negative Betaproteobacterium causing bacterial fruit blotch (BFB) on cucurbits; seedborne primary inoculum and T3SS-dependent pathogenesis.
- [[xanthomonas-euvesicatoria]]: Gram-negative bacterium (Group A; *X. euvesicatoria* pv. *euvesicatoria*); causal agent of bacterial spot on tomato and pepper, canonical model for T3SS and TALE biology.
- [[xanthomonas-perforans]]: Gram-negative bacterium (Group C; *X. euvesicatoria* pv. *perforans*); aggressive tomato and pepper bacterial spot pathogen producing bacteriocins and perforating shot-hole foliar lesions.
- [[xanthomonas-vesicatoria]]: Gram-negative bacterium (Group B); amylase-positive bacterial spot pathogen widely distributed across temperate and subtropical regions.
- [[xanthomonas-gardneri]]: Gram-negative bacterium (Group D; *X. hortorum* pv. *gardneri*); cold-adapted bacterial spot pathogen spreading clonally worldwide via hybrid seed trade.

## 2. Diseases (`wiki/diseases/`)
*Comprehensive disease records combining symptoms, epidemiology, and host impact.*

- [[bacterial-fruit-blotch]]: Destructive seedborne bacterial disease of watermelon and melon caused by *Acidovorax citrulli*; water-soaked foliar collapse and fruit blotches.
- [[bacterial-spot-tomato-pepper]]: Devastating foliar, stem, and fruit disease of solanaceous crops caused by a complex of four *Xanthomonas* species.

## 3. Hosts & Crops (`wiki/hosts/`)
*Host plants, model organisms (Arabidopsis, Nicotiana benthamiana), and crop resistance profiles.*

- [[citrullus-lanatus]]: Cultivated watermelon; cucurbit host susceptible to seedborne BFB, model for PTI intensification, NADPH oxidase ROS bursts, and callose deposition.
- [[solanum-lycopersicum]]: Cultivated tomato; premier dicot model organism and major vegetable host vulnerable to bacterial spot xanthomonads.
- [[capsicum-annuum]]: Sweet bell pepper and chili pepper; foundational genetic host model for *Bs1*–*Bs4* gene-for-gene resistance and executor R-gene mechanisms.

## 4. Molecular Biology & MPMI (`wiki/molecular-biology/`)
*Effectors, immune receptors (NLRs/PRRs), defense pathways, and virulence factors.*

- [[avrbs2]]: Conserved core T3SS effector with glycerophosphoryl diester phosphodiesterase activity essential for *Xanthomonas* virulence; recognized by pepper CC-NB-LRR Bs2.
- [[avrbs3]]: Prototypical Transcription Activator-Like Effector (TALE) from *X. euvesicatoria*; binds host promoter elements to induce cell hypertrophy or trigger executor *Bs3* resistance.
- [[cyclic-lipopeptides]]: Secondary metabolite families (surfactins, iturins, fengycins) synthesized by NRPS in plant-beneficial bacilli; membrane pore formation, antagonism, and ISR elicitation.

## 5. Research Methods & Protocols (`wiki/methods/`)
*Experimental protocols, inoculation assays, transformation techniques, and diagnostics.*

- [[in-planta-bacterial-transcriptomics]]: Dual-compartment protocol (epiphytic wash vs apoplastic infiltration, 70/5-µm filtration, host/pathogen ribodepletion) to capture spatiotemporal phytopathogen gene expression.
- [[spatial-separation-isr-assay]]: Standardized in vivo bioassay to uncouple direct bacterial antagonism from plant-mediated induced systemic resistance via root drenching, distal foliar challenge, and antioxidant enzyme profiling.

## 6. Management & Control (`wiki/management/`)
*Chemical MOA (FRAC), biocontrol agents, cultural practices, and IPM strategies.*

- [[bacillus-thuringiensis]]: Gram-positive entomopathogen and dual-action biopesticide; Cry $\delta$-endotoxin insecticidal proteins, ROS priming, SAR/ISR systemic immunity activation against pests and diseases in tomato.
- [[bacillus-velezensis]]: Gram-positive endospore-forming PGPR and biocontrol agent; secondary metabolite arsenal (CLPs, polyketides, bacteriocins), ISR elicitation, and safety profile.
- [[chemical-resistance-inducers]]: Plant resistance inducers (PRIs) including ASM/BTH (FRAC P01), salicylic acid, BABA, and chitosan; biochemical modes of action, defense gene transcription (*PR-1*, *PR-2*, *PR-4*, *CAT*), and synergy with *Bacillus* bioinoculants.
- [[copper-bactericides-and-resistance]]: Biochemical mode of action (FRAC M01), molecular mechanisms of plasmid-borne *copLAB* resistance, and 21st-century non-copper alternatives.

## 7. Core Concepts & Models (`wiki/concepts/`)
*Foundational frameworks (e.g., Zig-zag model, Invasion Model, Disease Triangle).*

- [[gene-for-gene-resistance]]: Classical Flor hypothesis and modern molecular paradigms (direct, indirect guard/decoy, and executor models) illustrated via the *Capsicum*-*Xanthomonas* pathosystem.
- [[induced-systemic-resistance]]: Systemic plant immune priming mediated by beneficial rhizobacteria (PGPR) via JA/ET pathways and microbial elicitors, compared against pathogen-triggered SAR.
- [[pamp-triggered-immunity]]: The evolutionarily ancient first active defense layer of plant immunity; cell-surface PRR perception, apoplastic NADPH oxidase ROS bursts, callose papillae, and microbial priming.
- [[rhizosphere-immune-modulation]]: Mechanisms of root immune quenching by beneficial rhizobacteria; bacterial gluconic acid secretion, microenvironmental acidification (pH 3.5–3.7), abolition of flg22-triggered ROS bursts, and prevention of growth-defense tradeoffs.
